# Sales Operating Model and Playbooks

## Motion selection

Choose the motion from the campaign diagnosis, not a preferred channel:

- **Warm/founder-led:** prioritize former customers, investors, advisors, alumni, partners, and mutual relationships where trust is the constraint.
- **Signal-led email:** use for a reachable, narrow ICP with a credible observed trigger and a clear offer.
- **Account-based motion:** use for high-value, complex deals; select a small named-account list and map multiple buying-group roles.
- **Intent-led motion:** prioritize accounts with first-party behavior or external signals such as hiring, launch, funding, stack change, compliance event, or competitor activity.
- **Inbound conversion:** route and respond quickly to trials, referrals, event attendees, or engaged visitors; timing and qualification matter more than cold cadence.
- **Partner/referral:** use where ecosystem credibility produces better access than a direct cold approach.
- **Human-approved X relationship motion:** use for visible founder/community conversations; it is not a high-volume cold-DM strategy.

## Playbook 1: ICP and account tiering

1. Define segment, job-to-be-done, buyer/user roles, blockers, geography, deal range, exclusions, and approved offer.
2. Build account tiers:
   - Tier 1: high fit plus current evidence of timing/need; manually reviewed.
   - Tier 2: clear fit plus one credible trigger.
   - Tier 3: fit only; nurture or hold.
3. Map Tier 1 buying groups instead of chasing one contact.
4. Score fit, intent, contactability, and priority separately.
5. Select a motion only after the score/evidence is inspectable.

## Playbook 2: signal research

1. Query only against an approved ICP/query plan.
2. Collect provider, URL, capture time, excerpt/fact, confidence, and the decision it supports.
3. Reject stale, ambiguous, duplicated, or unsourced evidence.
4. Mark contacts as unverified until their relevant channel is verified.
5. Store negative/exclusion evidence to avoid rediscovery.

## Playbook 3: email sequence

1. Write a concise, signal-led opener with one supported reason for relevance and one CTA.
2. Send a value-add follow-up that introduces genuinely distinct context; do not restate the opener.
3. Send a close-the-loop message only if policy permits and no terminal event exists.
4. Immediately stop on reply, bounce, unsubscribe, DNC, or meeting.
5. Review cohort outcomes by segment, offer, and signal before changing volume/cadence.

## Playbook 4: X relationship and DM

1. Prefer a contextual public interaction where appropriate.
2. Draft an individualized DM only with evidence, user approval, and validated provider policy.
3. Do not send identical, repeated, or high-volume unsolicited DMs.
4. Record the exact message and provider result as a touchpoint.
5. Route meaningful replies through conversation triage; do not negotiate autonomously.

## Playbook 5: reply triage and booking

1. Normalize inbound content and classify it.
2. Suppress immediately for unsubscribe/complaint.
3. Draft only bounded informational responses allowed by policy.
4. Escalate commercial, legal, security, procurement, custom scope, negative, or ambiguous cases.
5. For qualified interest, confirm attendee, email, timezone, and slot before creating an invite.
6. Persist the Calendar event ID and invitation lifecycle in the account timeline.
